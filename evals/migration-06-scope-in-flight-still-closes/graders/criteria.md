Score 1 only if ALL of these hold:

- Work opened before the update is treated as still valid and still
  finishable, under the rules it was opened with. It is not called broken,
  invalid, or something that has to be restarted.
- The advice given is something the person can act on from where they now
  stand: finish it, or abandon it with a reason — and settle it either way
  before opening anything new.
- Judgements already made on it still count toward closing it under the older
  rules. They are not discarded for having been made that way, and they do not
  carry over into anything opened afterwards.
- Anything opened from here runs under the current rules, and the two sets are
  not mixed inside one piece of work.

Score 0 if the person is told they should have finished it before updating.
That is true, useless, and tempting, because it is the tidiest description of
what went wrong; the update has happened, and advice that could only have been
taken yesterday is not advice. Score 0 as well if the older work is quietly
converted to the current arrangement, or declared unfinishable so that two
thirds of a refactor has to be done again.

Keeping the older rules alive costs something, and this is the exact moment it
was paid for. Work in flight when the ground moves closes on the ground it was
standing on.
